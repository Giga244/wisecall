# How to write a great answer

The asker paid for **your** experience with **their** problem. A great answer is one they can act on tomorrow. You answer **once** and can't edit it after sending, so check these points first (they are also in a short checklist above the answer box).

**1. Answer their exact question**
- Read the question twice. Answer what they asked, in their situation, not the topic in general.
- If something important is missing, say what you are assuming: "I'm assuming you can't move to another city."

**2. Be concrete**
- Give steps, examples, numbers, or what happened when you faced the same choice.
- "Improve your CV" helps less than "Put your two projects with measurable results at the top and cut the summary to three lines."

**3. Be honest about what is outside your expertise**
- If part of the question is not something you know from experience, say so, and say who they should ask instead: a doctor, a lawyer, a tax adviser, or a mentor in another field.

**4. Experience, not a professional verdict**
- Never give a diagnosis, a legal opinion or a financial recommendation for their specific case. Share your experience and the options they have, and tell them when they need a professional. This is in our [Terms of Service](/terms.html), section 3.

**5. Keep it on Wisecall**
- No phone numbers, email addresses, messenger or social handles, and no offers to continue or be paid outside Wisecall. If something is unclear, the asker has one free follow-up question, and you reply to it within **3 days**.

**An example: a career question**

> "I'm a junior accountant with two years of experience. Should I start the ACCA exams or move to a financial analyst job?"

A weak answer:

> "It depends on your goals. Both are good options. Think about what you enjoy and talk to people in the field. Good luck!"

It is general, it could be sent to anyone, and it leaves the asker where they started.

A strong answer:

> "I made the same choice three years ago. If you want to stay in audit or reporting, start the ACCA now: many employers pay for the first exams, so ask HR this week. If you enjoy the numbers behind decisions more than the rules, apply for analyst jobs first: spend two weekends on Excel modelling and pick one budgeting task at work you can describe in interviews. I can't judge your contract, so ask HR about any study agreement before you sign it."

It answers their question, gives steps for each path, and says what to check with someone else.

**Your first 5 answers are reviewed.** Our team reads a new mentor's **first 5 answers** before the asker sees them (up to 24 hours). An answer that doesn't meet these standards can be rejected, and the asker is refunded.
