# Residency abroad for Georgian doctors: Germany, the USA and other routes

Many Georgian medical graduates think about residency abroad — more specialties, structured training and career opportunities. It’s a long
and expensive road that often takes several years. This guide covers the general steps for the two most discussed destinations — Germany
and the USA — and what applies everywhere.

> Medical licensing rules differ by country, region (in Germany, by federal state) and year. This article is a general guide. Check every
> step with the official body.

## 1. Steps common to every country

- **Diploma and documents.** Your diploma, the supplement (subjects and hours), proof of internship/clinical practice, apostille and sworn
  translation. Collect a complete set from the start — you’ll need it again at later stages.
- **Your university’s status.** Many countries check whether your university is listed in a recognised directory (for example the World
  Directory of Medical Schools). Check yours.
- **Language.** Medical language in the clinic — not just a general level. This is often the longest stage.
- **Money.** Exams, translation, recognition fees, travel and living costs while you wait — plan in advance.
- **Status in Georgia.** Find out which documents and certificates you may need from Georgia’s Ministry of Health or your university
  [VERIFY with the relevant agency].

## 2. Germany

To work as a doctor in Germany you need an **Approbation** (full medical licence) or, temporarily, a **Berufserlaubnis** (temporary permit).
The general order:

1. **German** — generally B2 and a medical language exam (Fachsprachprüfung), usually run by the state medical chamber (Ärztekammer).
   Exact requirements vary by state.
2. **Apply for recognition** — to the competent authority in the state where you plan to work. They compare your training with German
   training.
3. **Equivalence check** — if significant differences are found, you may have to take a **Kenntnisprüfung** (knowledge exam).
4. **After Approbation** — look for a residency post (Weiterbildung), which in Germany is a salaried job.

Detailed official information: the German government portal “Make it in Germany” and the recognition portal “Anerkennung in Deutschland”. Visa
and residence questions: the German Embassy in Tbilisi.

## 3. The USA

To get into a US residency, an international medical graduate (IMG) usually needs:

1. **ECFMG certification** — verifies your education and requires passing USMLE exams. Check current requirements, including the assessment
   of communication and English, on the ECFMG website.
2. **USMLE exams** — Step 1 and Step 2 CK (and later Step 3). Step 1 is now pass/fail, so your Step 2 CK score and other factors carry more
   weight [VERIFY on usmle.org].
3. **US clinical experience** (USCE) — many programmes require or prefer it. Be careful with paid programmes that “promise” it — verify them.
4. **Applying through ERAS and the Match** — choosing programmes, recommendations, a personal statement, interviews, then the NRMP Match.
5. **Visa** — residents usually go on a J-1 (sponsored by ECFMG) or an H-1B — each has different conditions.

## 4. Other countries

Some Georgian doctors consider other European countries, the UK (GMC registration and the PLAB exam) or Israel. Each has its own system —
start with the country’s medical regulator’s official page, not an agent’s advert.

## Rough timeline

| Stage | Germany | USA |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation | Documents, German A1→B2 | Documents, prepare for USMLE Step 1 |
| Middle | Fachsprachprüfung, recognition application | Step 1, Step 2 CK, ECFMG, clinical experience |
| End | Kenntnisprüfung (if needed), Approbation, job | ERAS, interviews, Match, visa |

The whole process often takes several years — depending on your language level, exams and how complete your documents are.

## Planning time and money

This is a marathon and needs a good plan:

- **Working in parallel.** Many doctors prepare for the language or the USMLE while working in Georgia. It’s slower, but financially safer
  and keeps your clinical skills fresh.
- **Budget by stage.** Calculate each stage’s cost from official pages — exam fees, translation and apostille, recognition fees, language
  courses, travel and living costs. At some stages the cost comes all at once.
- **Mental readiness.** Waiting, rejection or retaking an exam are normal parts of it. Build a time buffer into your plan.
- **Family.** If your family is coming with you, check the visa and work rules for family members too.
- **A document folder.** Keep all originals, copies and translations in one place — digitally too. The same document is asked for again and
  again.

## How reliable is your information?

There’s a lot of information online, but always check the source and the date. Experiences shared in forums and groups are valuable, but the
rules may already have changed.

## Common mistakes

- **Underestimating the language** — speaking and documenting in the clinic is the hardest part.
- **Incomplete documents** — every missing certificate delays the process by months.
- **“Guaranteed residency” offers** — nobody can bypass the official process.
- **Choosing a country only by salary** — consider language, timelines and family.
