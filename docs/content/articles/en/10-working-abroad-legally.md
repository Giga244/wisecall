# Working abroad legally: how to go safely and avoid scams

Working abroad is an important source of income for many families. But an illegal job means being unprotected: the risk of unpaid wages,
deportation and a future entry ban. This guide covers how to go legally, how to check an employer and an agent, and what to do if something
goes wrong.

> Visa and work-permit rules differ by country and change often. The only reliable sources are the official pages of the destination
> country’s embassy or migration service and the relevant Georgian agencies.

## 1. Know the difference: visa-free travel ≠ the right to work

Georgian citizens can travel visa-free to the Schengen area for short stays, but **that does not give the right to work**. To work legally in
the EU or another country you usually need:

- a work visa or residence permit that allows work;
- often, a job offer and a procedure started by the employer;
- in some countries, a special seasonal-work visa or a quota.

Arriving visa-free and “sorting it out later” is the most common and dangerous mistake: breaking visa-free rules threatens all Georgians,
not just you.

## 2. State programmes

Georgia has **circular (temporary) labour migration** agreements and programmes with several countries — for example for seasonal work. This
is the safest route because the employer and conditions are checked in advance. See current programmes, countries and how to register on the
websites of Georgia’s labour and employment agencies [VERIFY: Ministry of Labour, Health and Social Affairs and the employment agency,
current addresses].

## 3. Check the employer and the agent

If you find a job through a private agency or an acquaintance:

1. **Does the employer exist?** Look the company up in the country’s official business register, and check its website, address and reviews.
2. **Is the agency legal?** In Georgia, labour-intermediary activity has registration rules — check whether the agency is registered with
   the relevant agency [VERIFY].
3. **A contract in advance** — salary, hours, housing, insurance, who pays for travel, how the contract ends. In a language you understand.
   Never sign a blank or unclear document.
4. **Check the visa type** — which visa you’re being given and whether it allows you to do this particular job.

## 4. Signs of fraud

Be careful if:

- they ask for a large upfront payment “for the visa” or “for placement” without an official receipt;
- they promise a “guaranteed” visa or job with a very high salary and minimal requirements;
- they suggest going on a tourist visa or visa-free and “sorting the documents” on arrival;
- they ask you to hand over your passport when you arrive;
- they don’t give you the contract in advance, or it’s only in a language you don’t know;
- communication happens only through a messenger and there is no official company contact.

If in doubt, don’t pay and check with an official agency. If you think there’s a risk of human trafficking or forced labour, contact the
police (112).

## 5. Before you go

- **Copies of your passport and contract** — digital, and left with your family.
- **Contact details of Georgia’s embassy/consulate** in the destination country — on geoconsul.gov.ge.
- **Consular registration** — use it if the service is available [VERIFY on geoconsul.gov.ge].
- **Insurance** — what it covers and who pays.
- **Basic language** — even a little, so you understand your rights.

## 6. Once you’re there

- keep your personal documents with you — don’t hand your passport to anyone “for safekeeping”;
- keep records of wages, working hours and every payment;
- find out the contacts of the local labour inspectorate and migrant-support organisations;
- if there’s a problem, contact the Georgian consulate — they can’t “fix” everything, but they’ll advise where to turn.

## How to prepare for a legal job

Your chances of a legal work visa grow with the things that give an employer a reason to start the procedure:

- **Language** — knowing the destination country’s language is often the main factor, especially in healthcare, services and construction.
- **Professional qualifications** — some countries have shortage-occupation lists, and visas for those occupations are relatively easier.
  The lists are on the country’s official immigration website.
- **Diploma recognition** — if your profession is regulated, start recognition before you leave.
- **A CV in the country’s format** — in Europe, the Europass format is common.
- **Job portals** — for example EURES (the EU’s job mobility portal) and countries’ official employment agencies. Check which vacancies are
  open to non-EU citizens.

## If you’re already abroad without the right status

If you’re already abroad and your status doesn’t allow work, or it has expired, don’t wait for it to “sort itself out”. The sooner you
contact the Georgian consulate or a licensed local immigration lawyer, the more options you have. Many countries have organisations that give
migrants free advice. Voluntary return often has fewer consequences than deportation — find out which rules apply in the specific country,
and make every decision based on an official source or a lawyer’s advice.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating visa-free travel as a right to work.**
- **Paying an agent without checking them.**
- **Leaving without a contract.**
- **Handing your passport to the employer.**
