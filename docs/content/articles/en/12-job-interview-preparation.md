# How to prepare for a job interview: questions, answers and talking about salary

Being nervous before an interview is normal — it happens to almost everyone. But good preparation reduces nerves and shows your real
abilities. This guide covers how to prepare step by step: from researching the company to talking about salary and the follow-up email.

## 1. Get to know the company and the role

Before the interview:

- read the **job description** carefully and highlight the main requirements;
- look at the company’s **website**, social media and recent news — what they do, who their clients are, what values they state;
- if possible, talk to someone who works or has worked there;
- find out who will interview you — HR, your future manager or the team.

This will help you answer “why do you want to work with us?” specifically, not generally.

## 2. Prepare your stories

Many questions are about your experience: “Tell us about a time when…”. The **STAR method** works well for these:

- **S — Situation:** briefly, what was happening;
- **T — Task:** what your responsibility was;
- **A — Action:** what **you** specifically did (not “we”);
- **R — Result:** what happened in the end and what you learned.

Prepare 5–6 stories that fit different questions: a success, a mistake and what you learned, a conflict in a team, a tight deadline, an
initiative, a difficult client.

## 3. Common questions

- **“Tell us about yourself”** — two minutes: who you are professionally, the main things you’ve done, why you’re here. Not a biography from
  birth.
- **“Why this role / this company?”** — connect your experience and goals to their work.
- **“What’s your weakness?”** — honest, but something you’re already working on, and how exactly.
- **“Why are you leaving your current job?”** — a forward-looking answer (growth, a new challenge), without criticising your employer.
- **“Where do you see yourself in a few years?”** — show that you’re growing and this role is part of your plan.

Don’t memorise answers word for word — have the main points and practise out loud.

## 4. The technical or practical part

In many fields the interview includes a practical task: a test, a case, a presentation or a take-home assignment.

- ask HR in advance what the format will be;
- revise the basics of your field;
- think out loud while doing the task — the approach is often assessed too;
- if you don’t know something, say so and show how you would find out.

## 5. Online interviews

- check your internet, camera, microphone and the software in advance;
- choose a quiet place with good light and a neutral background;
- look at the camera when you speak;
- have water, your CV and notes nearby — but don’t read your answers.

## 6. Talking about salary

Discussing salary is a normal part of the process, not an embarrassment.

- **Prepare:** research the market — similar vacancies, people in the field. Official average-wage statistics for Georgia are published by
  Geostat, though for a specific role they are only a general guide.
- **Give a range** whose lower end is acceptable to you.
- **Think about the whole offer:** insurance, bonus, remote work, a learning budget, holidays.
- **Ask for clarity:** is the figure net or gross? Because of income tax this makes a big difference.

## 7. Your questions for them

At the end you’ll almost always be asked: “Do you have any questions?”. Have 2–3:

- What does success look like in this role in the first three or six months?
- How does the team work, and who would my manager be?
- What are the next steps, and when will I hear back?

## 8. After the interview

- send a short thank-you email the same or the next day;
- note which questions you were asked and what you could have answered better;
- if you’re rejected, politely ask for feedback — you won’t always get it, but when you do it’s very useful.

## Multi-stage interviews

At large companies the interview often has several stages: a short HR call, a conversation with the manager, a practical task and a final
meeting. Each stage assesses something different: HR checks motivation and basic requirements, the manager checks professional skills, the
team checks collaboration. Ask at the start how many stages there are and over what timeframe, so you can plan your time. If you have another
offer in parallel, politely let them know — this often speeds things up. Between stages, note who you spoke to and what you were asked — these
notes will be very useful at the next stage.

## Checklist for the day before

- re-read the job ad and your CV — know what you wrote;
- go over your 5–6 STAR stories;
- prepare your “tell us about yourself” answer and time it;
- confirm the address or link, the time and the interview format;
- for an in-person interview, plan your route and arrive 10–15 minutes early;
- prepare clothes that fit the company’s style;
- prepare your salary range and your questions;
- sleep well — late-night preparation turns into morning tiredness.

## Common mistakes

- **Knowing nothing about the company.**
- **General answers** without specific examples.
- **Criticising a previous employer.**
- **Being unprepared for the salary question** — a figure that’s too low or unjustifiably high.
- **Having no questions** at the end of the interview.
