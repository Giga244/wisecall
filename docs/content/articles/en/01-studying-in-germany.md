# Studying in Germany: a step-by-step guide for Georgian students

Germany is one of the most popular destinations for Georgian students. At most public universities, tuition is either free or you only
pay a semester fee, and a German degree is respected across Europe. But the road is long and has many steps. This guide explains, briefly
and practically, where to start and where people usually go wrong.

> Every amount, deadline and requirement changes from year to year. Always check the current information on the official pages listed
> below.

## 1. Decide what you want to study, and why

Before you think about documents, answer three questions:

- **Which level?** Bachelor’s, master’s or PhD. With a Georgian school certificate you can’t always enter a bachelor’s programme directly —
  sometimes you first need to study at a university in Georgia for a while, or complete a preparatory course (Studienkolleg).
- **In which language?** Many programmes are taught in German, but there are quite a lot of English-taught master’s programmes.
- **Which kind of university?** A classic university (Universität) is more theoretical; a university of applied sciences (Hochschule /
  Fachhochschule) is more practical.

Use DAAD’s official programme finder to search: you can filter by level, language and field.

## 2. Check whether your diploma is recognised

The first obstacle is usually the recognition of a foreign diploma. In the **anabin** database you can see how a Georgian school
certificate and Georgian university degrees are assessed. If the rules are unclear, write directly to the university’s International
Office — they answer these questions every day.

## 3. Find out where and how to apply

Many universities receive applications from international students through **uni-assist**, an organisation that pre-checks your documents
and forwards them to the university. Others use their own portal. Every programme has its own deadline; applications for the winter
semester often close in early summer, but this varies by university.

**Tip:** make a spreadsheet with each programme’s deadline, language requirement, document list and application route.

## 4. Prepare for the language requirement

- **German-taught programme:** usually a certificate such as TestDaF or DSH — the exact level is on the programme page.
- **English-taught programme:** usually IELTS or TOEFL.

Learning the language and preparing for the test often takes the most time, so start early — ideally at least a year before you apply.

## 5. Prepare your documents

A typical list (always check the specific programme’s requirements):

1. School certificate or diploma and transcript — with a certified translation;
2. Language certificate;
3. Motivation letter;
4. CV;
5. Letters of recommendation (mostly for master’s programmes);
6. Copy of your passport.

Your **motivation letter** should be specific: why this programme, why this university, and how it connects to your experience and goals. A
generic text sent to ten universities is easy to spot.

## 6. Funding and the blocked account

Tuition may be free; living is not. For a student visa you normally have to show that you can cover your living costs. The most common way
is a **blocked account (Sperrkonto)**, where a year’s worth of money is deposited and released to you monthly. The amount changes every year —
check the current figure on the German Embassy’s website.

For scholarships, see DAAD’s scholarship database; some universities have their own.

## 7. The visa

Once you receive your admission letter (Zulassung), book a student-visa appointment at the German Embassy in Tbilisi. Waiting times can be
long, so learn the booking rules in advance and prepare your documents exactly according to the embassy’s list.

## 8. After you arrive

- Housing: places in student residences (Studentenwerk) fill up fast — apply as early as you can;
- Address registration (Anmeldung), health insurance, a bank account;
- Work on a student visa is allowed only within limits — check the rules in an official source.

## Rough timeline: what to do when

Exact dates depend on the programme, but the general order is:

| When (before your studies start) | What to do |
|---|---|
| About 18–12 months before | Choose a field and 5–10 programmes, check your diploma on anabin, start the language |
| 12–9 months before | Take the language test, draft your CV and motivation letter, ask for recommendation letters |
| 9–6 months before | Translate and certify documents, apply (via uni-assist or directly), look for scholarships |
| 6–3 months before | Receive answers, open the blocked account, book the visa appointment, look for housing |
| Last 3 months | Visa, insurance, tickets, housing confirmed, first month’s budget |

If you fall behind this timeline, it isn’t the end of the world: many programmes also admit students for the summer semester. What matters
is knowing in advance how long each step takes and starting the longest ones — language and documents — early.

## What it will cost

We deliberately don’t give exact figures here because they differ a lot by city and year. Plan for these costs and check each one in an
official source or with a student who lives there: semester fee, language test, translation and certification of documents, the uni-assist
fee, the visa fee, the blocked-account amount, a housing deposit and the first month’s rent, and health insurance.

## Common mistakes

- **Missing deadlines** — the most common problem. Translating and certifying documents takes time.
- **One generic motivation letter** for every programme.
- **Overestimating your language level** — starting a course is one thing; following lectures and passing exams is another.
- **Leaving without a financial plan** — the first months are usually the most expensive.
- **Paying an agent too much** for things you can do yourself on official websites.
