# Georgia’s national exams: how to prepare and choose the right university

For many families, the national exams are the main event of the year. The stress is understandable — the result decides where you study and
with what funding. But with the right plan, preparation becomes much more manageable. This guide covers how to plan the year, choose your
subjects and university, and what to watch out for when registering.

> Exam rules, subjects, registration deadlines and grant conditions can change every year. The only official source is the National
> Assessment and Examinations Center (naec.ge). Always check there.

## 1. Learn the rules first

Before you start preparing, find out on naec.ge:

- which subjects are compulsory and which are elective;
- which subjects your chosen faculty requires (this varies by university and programme);
- how the state study grant is calculated and what level of result it needs;
- registration deadlines and procedure — missing registration is a very expensive mistake.

If you’re also applying abroad, find out what they require — sometimes the national exam result isn’t needed at all, and sometimes another
test (for example the SAT or a language certificate) is.

## 2. Choose a direction and a university

Don’t decide only on “where will they take me”. Think about:

- what interests you and what you’re good at — which subjects you happily spend time on;
- what the profession is really like — talk to someone who works in it;
- the programme’s content — subjects, practice, exchange programmes; see the university website;
- accreditation — check with the National Center for Educational Quality Enhancement (eqe.ge);
- cost and funding options.

## 3. Make a study plan

1. **Diagnosis.** Take a past exam in each subject (naec.ge publishes samples and past papers). See where you are now and where you want to be.
2. **Topic list.** For each subject, list the topics and mark: I know it / partly / not at all.
3. **Weekly schedule.** Realistic — alongside school, other activities and rest. A little every day beats a lot on one day.
4. **Regular mock exams.** A full timed test every 2–4 weeks — so you also practise time management.
5. **Mistake notebook.** Write down every mistake — why you made it and what’s correct. Before the exam, this is your most valuable material.

## 4. Tutor or self-study?

A tutor isn’t required, but can help with structure, feedback and motivation. If you choose one:

- ask about their method — how they check progress, what homework they give;
- try one or two trial lessons;
- don’t trust a promise that they’ll “get you through” — look at their students’ real experience.

Self-study is possible too if you’re disciplined: official samples, textbooks and free online materials. The key is to have someone check your
essays and written tasks — a teacher, for example.

## 5. Filling in your priorities

Be careful when filling in your priorities: it’s as important as the exam itself.

- read on naec.ge how admission by priorities works;
- list programmes by what you **really want**;
- check that your chosen subjects match every programme;
- fill them in on time and double-check — sometimes a mistake can’t be corrected.

## 6. Health and stress

- sleep isn’t wasted time — your memory works while you sleep;
- physical activity, even a walk, reduces stress;
- talk to your parents about expectations — less pressure often means better results;
- if anxiety gets in the way of daily life, talk to a psychologist — that’s normal.

## For parents: how to help

A parent’s role is big, but help sometimes turns into pressure. A few tips:

- **Help with the plan, not with control** — make the schedule together, but don’t check every hour.
- **Let your child choose the faculty** — your experience is valuable, but they are the one who will study the profession.
- **Don’t compare with others** — “the neighbour’s child got this many points” doesn’t motivate.
- **Take care of the basics** — sleep, food, a calm home.
- **Discuss money in advance** — what happens if there’s no grant or only a partial one; this removes uncertainty for your child.
- **Read the official information together** — two people check the rules and deadlines on naec.ge more easily than one.

## Rough timeline

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| The summer / autumn before | Learn the rules, choose a direction, diagnostic tests, plan |
| Autumn–winter | Systematic preparation, a full mock exam every month |
| Registration period | Register following naec.ge’s instructions — check the deadline |
| Spring | Timed tests, revising your mistakes |
| After the exams | Carefully fill in your priorities within the official deadlines |

## If the result isn’t what you wanted

It isn’t the end. You can check whether there is an appeal option and its deadline (naec.ge), consider other programmes or universities,
vocational education, or retake next year with a better plan. Many successful people didn’t get where they wanted on the first try. What
matters is analysing what didn’t work and planning the next step calmly.

## Common mistakes

- **Missing the registration or priorities deadline.**
- **Only reading theory** without solving tests.
- **Choosing a faculty because of others’ opinions** — you’ll be studying this profession for years.
- **Learning everything in the last month** — exhaustion hurts results.
